# Planispheres

A planisphere, or star wheel, combines a rotating star map with a sleeve whose window represents your local horizon. Line up a date on the wheel with a time on the sleeve. The stars inside the window are the ones above the horizon then. Turn the wheel forward a few hours to see which constellations will rise while you’re outside.

## Make one for your latitude

Choose your observing latitude in Lyncean Education’s generator, or use its **Use Current Location** button. Download the PDF and print it on cardstock. It includes labeled and blank versions of a double-sided star wheel. The same guide shows how the author adapted the paper design for laser engraving. [Printable Planispheres generator and build guide](https://lyncean.education/projects/astronomy/printable-planispheres/)

Cut out the two star maps and glue them back to back, aligning the month markings on both sides. Assemble the sleeve with its spacers so the disk can turn freely. Slide the disk in with its northern stars facing the northern window.

To try it outside, set the date and hour, face north, and hold the north side in front of you. Turn around and use the south side to explore the opposite horizon. If you travel to a different latitude, keep the star wheel and print a sleeve for the new location.
