SalvageCorps works with local businesses to recover usable discards, including from commercial dumpsters where we have permission. We bring fixtures, hardware, and other materials we can reuse or repair to the [Recycling and Upcycling Depots](/projects/recycling-and-upcycling-depots/) for sorting and storage.

We ask stores to set aside safe surplus food for direct pickup. Volunteers and food-sharing partners verify where the food comes from and how stores handle it, and keep it at safe temperatures during transport.

**A blanket “we’d be liable” policy is no excuse for trashing good food.** The [Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act](https://www.usda.gov/sites/default/files/documents/usda-good-samaritan-faqs.pdf) protects stores from liability tied to the age or condition of apparently wholesome food they donate in good faith to a nonprofit serving people in need. It also protects qualifying grocers that donate directly to people in need at no charge. A [“best if used by” date marks quality](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/Can-I-keep-meat-or-poultry-that-has-a-use-by-date-in-my-refrigerator-until-the-use-by-date), and food can still qualify for donation after that date if it meets applicable standards. Stores still have to follow health rules, and the law excludes gross negligence and intentional misconduct. Set the food aside and arrange a pickup. There’s a legal path to getting it to people who need it.

We arrange regular pickups of spent coffee grounds from cafés and bring them to land projects. The [Floramancers](/guilds/floramancers/) mix the grounds with dry leaves or other carbon-rich material to make compost that helps the soil retain water.
