([Extension](https://extension.unr.edu/publication.aspx?PubID=3222&utm_source=chatgpt.com), [CALS Communications & Cyber Technologies](https://apps.cals.arizona.edu/arboretum/taxon.aspx?id=207&utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Coloma gets plenty hot in July and August, but **spring is considerably cooler than a real date-growing region**. So I'd regard dates there as an interesting experimental crop, rather than something I'd plant acres of.

### Nevada City

Nevada City flips the picture somewhat. The city itself is around zone 9a, but the surrounding ZIP spans enormous topographic variation-roughly zones 7b through 9b-and you're around 2,500 feet rather than Coloma's ~760 feet. Summers are also much cooler: roughly **87°F average July/August highs**, compared with ~94°F in Coloma. ([Plant Maps](https://www.plantmaps.com/95959?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That makes **yaupon the clear winner**. Winter cold isn't a serious limitation and summer warmth is sufficient. With irrigation, I would expect it to do very well.

Black Mission fig is also quite plausible. A protected south-facing site with good cold-air drainage could produce excellent figs. The issue is occasional winter damage and late spring frost; Mission isn't among the cold-hardiest figs. Fully dormant common figs can tolerate around 15-20°F, but Mission itself is relatively cold-sensitive. ([Ask IFAS - Powered by EDIS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/MG214?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) So I wouldn't plant it in a frost pocket.

**Mission olive is more interesting.** It may grow, and Mission is unusually cold tolerant, but UC's Central Sierra recommendations stop olives at about **1,500 feet**, substantially below Nevada City. ([UC Agriculture and Natural Resources](https://www.ucanr.edu/site/ucce-central-sierra-agriculture/olive-production?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) The problem isn't simply killing the tree: Mission is a relatively late-maturing olive, and you need the fruit mature before damaging autumn frosts. UC notes that serious wood damage starts becoming an issue below roughly 15°F and that fruit should mature before fall freezes. ([UC Agriculture and Natural Resources](https://ucanr.edu/node/131008/printable/print?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

So at Nevada City I'd call Mission olive **a worthwhile trial on a warm south/southwest slope**, but not something I'd assume will behave like a Sacramento Valley olive orchard.

Date palms are the weakest candidate. You could probably establish a palm in a protected microclimate, but **producing high-quality dates reliably is unlikely** because there simply isn't enough sustained heat.

### Kingman

Kingman is the really interesting experimental site.

It averages about **99°F in July**, ~97°F in August and ~91°F in September, and receives only about eight inches of precipitation annually. So its growing-season heat is excellent. But at about 3,400 feet, the winter climate is dramatically colder than a classic date/olive desert. The 1991-2020 climate record averages roughly **62 nights at or below freezing per year**, USDA puts it around zone 8b, and the historical record includes single-digit temperatures. ([Global Futures Lab](https://globalfutures.asu.edu/azclimate/wp-content/uploads/sites/35/2025/06/Kingman.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com), [UA Cooperative Extension](https://extension.arizona.edu/publication/arizona-climate-zones-and-their-application-growing-plants?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That creates some unusual combinations.

**Yaupon:** I think this is quite feasible. Its normal hardiness range includes zone 8b, and its drought, alkaline-soil and soil-texture tolerance are unusually broad. ([Plant Toolbox](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/ilex-vomitoria/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) With drip irrigation and establishment care, I would take this seriously as a high-desert yaupon trial.

**Black Mission fig:** also plausible. Summer is more than hot enough. Winter injury is the issue. A severe Kingman freeze could kill above-ground wood, particularly on Mission. But figs recover aggressively from roots, so an orchard managed more like multistemmed shrubs could be resilient. For a small experimental planting I'd absolutely try it; for dependable commercial yields, I'd want several years of site-specific data.

**Mission olive:** this one might actually be one of your more interesting high-desert experiments. Mission is specifically classified by UC sources as cold hardy relative to other important California olives, and UC freeze research found it substantially hardier than Manzanillo. ([UC ANR](https://my.ucanr.edu/repository/view.cfm?article=70024+&groupid=1&search=+&utm_source=chatgpt.com), [UCANR Class](https://class.ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2010-07/27184.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

Kingman's average extreme winter temperature is nevertheless right around the danger line. That means I would expect:

- most normal winters: fine;
- moderate freezes: some foliage/twig damage;
- unusually severe freezes: potentially major wood loss;
- fruit maturation: generally enough summer heat, but harvest timing matters because autumn freezes can arrive early.

A **south- or southwest-facing slope above the valley floor** would change my opinion substantially in its favor. Cold-air drainage could easily represent several degrees of protection.

Dates are almost the mirror image: **Kingman has enough summer heat but too much winter risk**. Date palms grow best where winter temperatures remain above about 20°F, though established palms can tolerate short excursions into the mid/lower teens. ([Extension](https://extension.unr.edu/publication.aspx?PubID=3222&utm_source=chatgpt.com)) Kingman routinely puts you close to that boundary and occasionally far below it.

So I'd plant a handful of early-ripening date cultivars experimentally, but not build a date orchard around them.

### Barstow

Barstow is where the date palm suddenly makes sense.

The climate is extremely hot and dry: roughly **103°F average July highs, 101°F August, 95°F September**, around 4-5 inches of annual rainfall, and about 121-150 days per year above 86°F. It's mostly USDA 8b-9a, with warmer microsites reaching 9b. ([Plant Maps](https://www.plantmaps.com/92311?utm_source=chatgpt.com), [Barstow, CA](https://www.barstowca.org/residents/about-barstow/weather-climate?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

That is much closer to actual date-country conditions.

**Dates:** very promising. Winter freeze is still a nonzero concern-especially at exposed low-lying sites-but the combination of prolonged heat, very low summer precipitation and irrigation is fundamentally right. I'd prefer early-to-midseason cultivars rather than extremely late dates. Established palms and a frost-draining site would help considerably.

**Mission olives:** also very promising. They're cold hardy enough for most Barstow winters, there's ample heat for ripening, humidity is low, and irrigation solves the main water limitation. The thing I'd watch is **soil drainage/salinity**, not climate.

**Mission figs:** excellent climatic fit once established. The same dry heat that favors dates favors ripe figs, and irrigation lets you avoid the small, dry fruit that severe water stress would otherwise produce. Arizona Extension specifically discusses Black Mission as a useful cultivar for hot desert production and emphasizes deep irrigation during summer. ([UA Cooperative Extension](https://extension.arizona.edu/publication/growing-common-figs-low-desert?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Yaupon:** surprisingly plausible as well. It isn't naturally a Mojave Desert plant, but its physiological tolerance is broad-drought, alkaline soil, full sun, and zones covering Barstow's winter temperatures. ([Ask IFAS - Powered by EDIS](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/ST311?utm_source=chatgpt.com)) I'd expect it to consume noticeably more irrigation than the olives once you try to maintain high leaf production, though.

### For the kind of project you've been talking about

If the objective is **testing these as specialty crops and selecting locally adapted genetics**, I would actually see the four sites as complementary:

**Coloma:** Mission olive + Mission fig + yaupon baseline/control site  
**Nevada City:** cold-elevation selection pressure, especially yaupon/fig/olive  
**Kingman:** high-desert selection trial for yaupon + Mission olive + fig  
**Barstow:** date-production site plus extreme-hot/dry trials of all four

And there's a potentially very interesting **Coloma → Kingman selection program for Mission olive and yaupon**. Coloma gives you high productivity under relatively easy conditions; Kingman lets you select hard for winter cold, intense solar radiation, alkaline soil, low humidity and irrigation efficiency.

For your high-desert agriculture idea, **Mission olive and yaupon are the two I'd be most excited about testing in Kingman**, with Mission figs close behind. Dates there are much more of a gamble. In Barstow, I'd reverse that and make dates one of the headline crops.

### Black Mission fig - feasible, but interestingly marginal

### Yaupon - good

Unlike Black Mission fig or date palms, the normal winter temperatures aren't pushing yaupon particularly close to its survival limit. The more interesting selection pressure would be:

**extreme aridity + intense solar radiation + alkaline/high-desert soil + large diurnal temperature swings.**

With irrigation, I'd expect establishment to be quite feasible.

For the project you've been talking about, **Lucerne Valley may actually be a better yaupon selection site than Barstow**, because you're testing true high-desert adaptation rather than just extreme heat tolerance. 

### Dates - much more interesting than I initially would have guessed

I would put Lucerne Valley **between Kingman and Barstow**, and probably somewhat closer to Kingman.

The summer temperature profile is actually excellent for dates. The problem is spring/winter.

Date palms prefer winters above about 20°F, although mature palms can survive short excursions into the lower teens. They also need enormous accumulated heat to mature high-quality fruit. ([Extension](https://extension.unr.edu/publication.aspx?PubID=3222&utm_source=chatgpt.com), [UC Agriculture and Natural Resources](https://www.ccfruitandnuts.ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2019-09/Ag_Briefs81576.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**Tree survival: quite plausible**  
**Getting edible dates: plausible**  
**Reliable premium-quality date crop: uncertain**

### Date palms: 🟡/🔴

Bishop is almost the reverse of most marginal date-growing climates.

There is **plenty of summer heat**. July averages ~96°F, and Bishop commonly exceeds 100°F. ([Plantmaps](https://www.plantmaps.com/en/clim/f/us/california/bishop/climate-data?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

**surviving palm:** plausible
